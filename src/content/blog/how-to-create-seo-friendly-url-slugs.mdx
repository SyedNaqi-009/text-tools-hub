# How to Create SEO-Friendly URL Slugs — Best Practices & Tools

A **URL slug** is the final, readable segment of a web address that identifies a specific page on a website. For example, in the URL:

`https://texttoolshub.com/tools/slug-generator`

The slug is `slug-generator`.

While seemingly minor, URL slugs play a key role in search engine optimization (SEO), user trust, and social sharing. A clean, descriptive slug immediately tells both search bots and human visitors what the page is about before they even click.

In this guide, we cover the anatomy of an optimal URL slug, common pitfalls to avoid, and how to automate slug creation for large publishing workflows.

---

## 5 Rules for Crafting High-Performing URL Slugs

### 1. Always Use Lowercase Letters
Always format your URL slugs in lowercase. Unix-based web servers (such as Apache, NGINX, and Linux containers) treat uppercase and lowercase URLs as distinct addresses:
- `example.com/Blog/Slug`
- `example.com/blog/slug`

If a user or external site links with different capitalization, it can create duplicate content issues or trigger 404 errors. Standardizing on lowercase addresses keeps site architecture clean and predictable.

### 2. Separate Words with Hyphens, Not Underscores
Major search engines (including Google) officially treat hyphens (`-`) as word separators. In contrast, underscores (`_`) are often interpreted as joining words together:
- `how-to-bake-bread` ➔ Interpreted as four distinct keywords: `how`, `to`, `bake`, `bread`.
- `how_to_bake_bread` ➔ May be interpreted as a single compounded token: `how_to_bake_bread`.

Always use hyphens to ensure search crawlers parse individual keywords properly.

### 3. Strip Accents and Diacritics (Unicode Transliteration)
Accented letters (such as `é`, `ñ`, `ü`, and `ç`) frequently become corrupted when copied into plain-text emails, social media apps, or older browser address bars, turning into ugly percent-encoded strings (like `%C3%A9`).

Convert accented characters to their closest ASCII equivalents:
- `"café"` ➔ `"cafe"`
- `"über-cool"` ➔ `"uber-cool"`
- `"piñata"` ➔ `"pinata"`

### 4. Remove Stop Words and Punctuation
Search algorithms ignore basic grammatical filler words ("a", "the", "and", "in", "of") when indexing web addresses. Trimming these stop words keeps your URLs short and focused on your core keywords:
- **Article Title**: *"The Ultimate Guide on How to Clean Your Text Formatting for Free"*
- **Optimized Slug**: `clean-text-formatting-guide`

### 5. Keep Slugs Concise (3 to 5 Words)
Shorter URLs are easier to remember, look cleaner on social media cards, and are less likely to be clipped in mobile search results. Aim for a sweet spot of 3 to 5 descriptive words that capture your primary search intent.

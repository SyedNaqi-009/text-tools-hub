# How to Estimate Reading Time for Your Blog Posts and Articles

Have you noticed how popular publications like Medium, Substack, and major tech blogs display a small badge at the top of every article: **"5 min read"**?

That simple estimate has a measurable impact on reader behavior. In an era of shrinking attention spans and information overload, giving visitors an upfront estimate of the time commitment helps them decide whether to read immediately or bookmark for later, significantly reducing bounce rates.

In this article, we look at the research behind average reading speeds and show you how to calculate accurate reading and speaking times for your own content.

---

## The Science of Reading Speeds

How fast do people actually read? Research into adult literacy and cognitive processing provides clear benchmarks:

### 1. Silent Reading Speed
In a landmark 2019 meta-analysis analyzing hundreds of reading studies across thousands of participants, researchers found that the average adult reading English prose silently reads at:

> **238 words per minute (WPM)** for non-technical prose.

- For light fiction or conversational blog posts, reading speeds often reach **250–300 WPM**.
- For dense technical documentation, legal agreements, or scientific papers, comprehension requires a slower pace of **150–200 WPM**.

### 2. Spoken / Presentation Speed
Reading silently is much faster than reading aloud. When someone gives a keynote, reads a script, or hosts a podcast, their delivery speed typically drops to:

> **130 to 160 words per minute (WPM)**.

Speaking any faster causes audience fatigue and reduces listener comprehension, which is why professional speechwriters deliberately pace scripts to this narrower range.

---

## The Mathematical Formula for Reading Time

Calculating reading time is a straightforward formula:

```text
Reading Time (minutes) = Total Word Count / Reading Speed (WPM)
```

For example, for a 1,200-word article:

```text
1,200 words / 238 WPM ≈ 5.04 minutes
```

Rounding to the nearest minute gives a clean **5 min read** badge.

If the calculated time is under 60 seconds (for example, a 150-word announcement), it is standard publishing practice to label it as **"1 min read"** or **"< 1 min read"**.

---

## Why Displaying Reading Time Boosts Engagement

Publishing an estimated reading time provides three major user experience benefits:

1. **Reduces Anxiety & Bounce Rates**: When users land on an article without knowing its length, they often feel overwhelmed by endless scrolling and leave. A "3 min read" badge reassures them that the content is quick and digestible.
2. **Encourages Completion**: Readers who know an article requires only 4 minutes are more likely to finish reading the piece in a single session.
3. **Improves Content Pacing for Presenters**: If you are drafting a keynote or webinar script, calculating speaking time prevents your presentation from running over your allocated slot.
